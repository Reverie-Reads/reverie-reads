# Reader validation: first-use and return loop

Status: **materials ready; five observed pilot sessions remain**. No invitation has been sent and no
participant data has been collected. The owner must authorize any outreach separately.

## Decision this work must support

The current product program is coherent enough to test. The next decision is whether a
series-heavy collection keeper can bring a real library into Reverie, understand what it gives
them, make one useful decision from their own books, and return to continue that reading life.

The five-session pilot diagnoses the method and severe product blockers. It cannot establish
market demand from five people. If the protocol works, expand to the strategy sample: 20–25
problem interviews, at least 10 artifact walkthroughs, and 15 consented concierge imports.

Do not use this work to ask whether someone likes the visual design or the idea. Ask for a recent
real example, observe the existing workaround, and count what the reader can do without coaching.

## Pilot reader mix

Recruit five readers who each own or manage at least 100 books, follow at least three active
series, and currently use a reading app plus a second workaround such as notes, a spreadsheet, a
wishlist, or memory. Aim for variation that can expose different failures:

1. a reader with a large physical collection and series gaps;
2. a reader mixing physical, ebook, audiobook, borrowed, and wishlist copies;
3. a frequent rereader with notes or reading history worth preserving;
4. a privacy-sensitive reader who avoids public reading profiles;
5. a reader with a Goodreads or StoryGraph export and a messy or duplicated catalog.

One reader may satisfy several descriptions. Record the actual mix; do not recruit a convenient
general-reader sample and relabel it afterward. At least three participants must arrive with a
real export ready so the pilot can measure import and time to value; the other two may begin with
manual Add, scan, or the prepared fictional library.

Use these screener questions before scheduling:

1. About how many books do you personally keep track of?
2. How many unfinished series are you actively following?
3. Which app or catalog do you use, and what do you keep somewhere else?
4. Can that tool export your library, and would you be comfortable importing it locally during a
   screen-shared session?
5. Which phone or computer would you naturally use for this task?

The screener selects a relevant mix; it does not collect book titles or ask whether the person
likes the proposed product.

## Privacy and consent boundary

- Explain the session purpose, duration, recording choice, and data retained before beginning.
- Recording is off by default. Obtain explicit consent before recording audio, video, or screen.
- A participant may use a prepared fictional library. If they choose their own library, import in
  their browser or by screen share. Do not ask them to email a raw export.
- Never copy titles, searches, notes, reviews, ratings, moods, or book-level behavior into the
  research record. Quote only with separate permission and remove identifying book details.
- Record library size, import size, and series depth as buckets. Keep the session key separate from
  contact details. Delete working files at the end of the session.
- A participant may stop, skip a task, or request deletion of the session record at any time.

Use this opening:

> I am testing whether Reverie helps you work with your own library. I am testing the product, not
> you. Please think aloud. I will stay quiet unless you are blocked; if I help, I will record that
> the step was assisted. We will not retain your book titles, notes, searches, or ratings. You can
> stop or skip anything at any time.

## Session one: 45 minutes

### 1. Current behavior before Reverie — 10 minutes

Ask the reader to show the actual shelf, app, list, spreadsheet, or notes they use now.

- Tell me about the last time you could not decide what to read from books you already had.
- Tell me about the last series gap, duplicate purchase, forgotten copy, or lost reading note that
  changed what you did.
- What did you use to solve it? How long did it take? What happened afterward?
- What makes you trust or distrust a library app with this information?
- Which current tool would you refuse to stop using, and why?

Do not describe Reverie until this walkthrough is complete.

### 2. First impression — 3 minutes

Start on the public landing page. Ask the reader to explain what they think Reverie does, whose
library it is for, what appears private, and what they expect the primary action to do. Record
their words before answering questions.

### 3. Bring in a library — 10 minutes

Ask the reader to begin from the path they would naturally choose: guest example, file import,
search, scan, or manual Add. For a prepared export, start the time-to-value clock when the file is
ready to choose. Observe whether they can:

- understand what the import will add or change;
- recognize and resolve a duplicate or uncertain match;
- distinguish owned, borrowed, wishlist, format, and reading state;
- find one imported book again after the operation completes;
- understand what stayed private and how to export or remove it.

Stop and classify any apparent loss, overwrite, cross-account exposure, or misleading success as a
severity-one trust blocker. Do not continue the session past a suspected privacy or data-loss issue.

### 4. Reach a useful decision — 12 minutes

Give the outcome and let the reader choose the route:

> Use Reverie to decide what you want to do next with a book you already have access to.

A meaningful outcome is one of these completed actions:

- chooses and starts or saves a next read from an owned or borrowed copy;
- finds and resolves a real series gap or incorrect series category;
- corrects a meaningful copy, cover, edition, or metadata problem;
- creates a useful near-term reading plan;
- retrieves a past reading note or history they wanted to keep.

Do not count opening pages, changing a room, browsing a feed, or importing books by itself. Stop
the time-to-value clock at the first meaningful outcome.

### 5. Unprompted exploration and close — 10 minutes

Ask what they expected but could not find, what they would return for, what feels unsafe or too
laborious, and what they would keep using elsewhere. After they have experienced value, show the
actual current Free/Plus boundary and ask what would have to be true for payment to make sense.
Record current-value objections; do not promise unbuilt benefits or treat hypothetical willingness
to pay as payment proof.

## Return check: 7–10 days

Do not prescribe a reminder inside the product. At the agreed follow-up, ask the reader to open
Reverie and continue without a refresher. Observe whether they can find the saved choice, update or
finish a read, change a plan, revisit Reflect, or make another decision from their library.

Record whether the return was spontaneous or prompted. A prompted appointment is evidence that the
workflow remains understandable; it is not retention. A separate unprompted return is stronger
evidence and should be recorded separately.

## Observation vocabulary

Every task receives one result:

- **Independent:** completed without moderator direction after the task prompt.
- **Assisted:** completed after any hint, route name, control location, or recovery instruction.
- **Failed:** abandoned, completed incorrectly, or required the moderator to take control.
- **Not attempted:** unavailable or outside the participant's chosen path.

Record the first obstruction, the recovery attempt, support minutes, and whether the participant
noticed the result. Do not convert an assisted completion into independent because it eventually
worked.

## Five-session process gate

Proceed to the larger validation sample only when:

- at least four of five readers bring in or add books without a severity-one trust blocker;
- median time to first value is under 15 minutes for readers with an export ready;
- at least three of five complete a meaningful outcome independently;
- no more than one reader is blocked primarily by the privacy/trust model; and
- the follow-up can distinguish prompted comprehension from an unprompted return.

Any privacy exposure or destructive data-loss defect blocks recruitment expansion until fixed and
reverified. Three repeated failures at the same step define a product problem even if the five-person
threshold still passes. Five sessions provide no pricing or retention verdict; use the larger
strategy sample for those decisions.

## Review cadence

After each session, record the result before discussing solutions. After sessions two and five,
group repeated obstructions by outcome and severity. Fix a release blocker immediately; otherwise
finish the five-session pilot before changing the flow so later observations remain comparable.

The outcome of this task is a decision record: expand the protocol, repair a named blocker and
repeat the pilot, or narrow the target reader. It is not a feature wish list.
